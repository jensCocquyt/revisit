Security advisory: certificate validation bypass in libhandshake 2.x (CVE-2026-31337)

Severity: critical (CVSS 9.1). Affected: libhandshake 2.0.0 through 2.8.3. Fixed: 2.8.4 and 3.1.1. Published 12 August 2026; embargo lifted after coordinated disclosure with major downstream distributions.

A flaw in the resumption path of the TLS handshake allows an attacker who can observe one legitimate session to craft a resumption ticket that skips certificate chain validation entirely on reconnect. Clients that enable session resumption — the default in 2.x — will silently accept an attacker-controlled server certificate on the resumed connection. Servers are not affected; this is a client-side validation bypass.

Exploitation requires a network position between client and server, but no cryptographic breakthrough: a proof of concept is public and fits in eighty lines. We are aware of scanning activity but have no confirmed reports of exploitation in the wild at publication time.

Remediation: upgrade to 2.8.4 or 3.1.1 immediately. If you cannot upgrade this week, disabling session resumption (set handshake_resumption = off) fully mitigates the flaw at a measurable latency cost on reconnect-heavy workloads. Vendored copies are the long tail here — audit anything that statically links libhandshake, including container base images built before 12 August.

Detection: resumed sessions negotiated with a certificate fingerprint different from the original session are the signature; the advisory includes a script that checks connection logs for that pattern retroactively.
